# Changelog

## 1.2 - the big overnight update

Everything below was added or fixed in this release, grouped by area.

### Latest: why rivals crashed, traced and fixed
A new tool, `tools/incident-causes.mjs`, records for every marshal recovery what happened to that car in the ten seconds before it, and what first damaged it. It showed that rivals almost never ran off on their own on the racing line; the recoveries came from three specific faults:

- **Defending rivals braked for the wrong corner.** The speed profile is for the racing line, which uses the whole width of the road. A rival that moved to the inside to cover or block kept braking for that speed on a much tighter arc, arrived too fast, ran out of steering lock and spun into the wall, then limped round being recovered again and again. Drivers now work out the corner speed of the path they are actually on (`AIDriver.offLineV`, switch `RC_TUNE.offLine`) and brake for that. A defender or an attacker on the inside is now slower through the corner than a car on the line, which is also how it works in real racing.
- **A recovered car could be stranded outside the pit wall.** A car that was put back on the road while heading for its pit box stayed in the "pitting" state on the circuit side of the pit wall and drove at the wall until it was recovered again, every nine seconds. It now rejoins in the pit lane only if it was already in it, and otherwise abandons the stop.
- **Badly damaged cars spun on the pit entry.** A car with a puncture, or with its wings off and its tyres worn through, took the entry ramp at the limiter speed and spun. It now crawls in at 60 % of the limit until it is straight in the lane. Healthy cars are unaffected.

Measured on Northfield (20 cars, 5 laps, triple tyre wear so that everybody has to stop), summed over the seeds given, with the off-line corner speed switched off and on in otherwise identical code:

| Level | Seeds | Car contacts | Wall hits | Marshal recoveries | Position changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elite | 8 | 57 → 26 | 152 → 103 | 48 → 15 | 1069 → 848 |
| Legend | 4 | 44 → 21 | 124 → 24 | 40 → 14 | 640 → 455 |
| F1 | 8 | 64 → 58 | 218 → 185 | 76 → 71 | 1042 → 958 |
| Legend, Vettore | 4 | 46 → 36 | 32 → 48 | 13 → 7 | 530 → 519 |

What this does and does not show: Elite and Legend are clearly cleaner. F1 level is not: the difference there is inside the noise (one set of four seeds was worse, the other much better). There are 10 to 25 % fewer position changes, partly because fewer cars are crashing out of the way and partly because a car off the line is now honestly slower.

- **Rivals drive to their weaker axle.** What was left at F1 level was cars on worn tyres still being driven at F1 pace: a driver judged his grip from the average of the four tyres, but a car with dead rears and fair fronts lets go at the rear. A rival lapping alone at F1 level on Northfield spun on its fifth lap for exactly this reason. Drivers now take the weaker of the two axles (`RC_TUNE.weakAxle`). On top of the change above, F1 level over the same 8 seeds: car contacts 58 → 51, wall hits 185 → 80, marshal recoveries 71 → 32, finishers 152 → 159 of 160. Elite over 4 seeds: contacts 7 → 5, wall hits 20 → 14, recoveries 5 → 4, finishers 77 → 80 of 80. The cost, and it is a real one: a rival's first laps on fresh tyres are unchanged (the pace table is the same to a hundredth), but it now fades as its tyres go. Alone on mediums at Legend level on Northfield, lap six is 1:14.9 instead of 1:13.9. The rivals are slower late in a stint than they were; in exchange they stop crashing.

- **Rival pace next to your own.** The race setup screen now says how fast the rivals lap the chosen circuit at the chosen difficulty (measured, the README pace table), compares it with your record there, and offers **Match my pace**, which sets the level whose rivals lap as fast as you do. `tests/pacetable.mjs` keeps the table in step with the circuits and difficulty presets; `JSON=file node tools/pace-table.mjs` regenerates it.
- **Checked by eye for the first time:** the sector bridges and the marshal posts with their yellow flags were shipped without ever being looked at; both now have screenshots and render correctly. Still not seen in a screenshot: bottoming sparks. Still not heard by anybody: the audio mix.

### New game modes
- **Driving school**: seven short lessons (launch and top speed, clean braking, clean laps, DRS, slipstream and overtaking, a pit stop, wet driving), each on a suitable circuit with an on-screen checklist and a radio tip. Progress is saved; `tests/school.mjs` drives every lesson headlessly and `tests/e2e.mjs` covers the screens.
- **Qualifying** (main menu: *Qualify & race*, or the *Qualifying session first* toggle in the race setup): a 3-minute session with all 20 cars on track. Cars leave the grid 2.4 s apart, collisions are off, DRS is free, the session ends when the clock runs out and each car completes the lap in progress. The classification screen shows every car's best lap and gap; *Start race from Pn* builds the race grid from the result (player and AI alike).
- **Career mode**: choose a team (the weaker the car, the tougher the start), difficulty, calendar length (4 or 8 rounds) and race distance (Sprint / Standard / Grand Prix). Each race pays prize money by finishing position, with a bonus for hitting the team objective and for the fastest lap. Money buys car upgrades (power unit, aerodynamics, chassis & tyres, six levels each). After the final round you get a season recap (position, constructors' result, wins, podiums, a season bonus) and contract offers from better teams if you earned them. Difficulty rises a little each season. All saved in `localStorage`.
- **Championship** now uses the same season framework: calendar, drivers' and constructors' standings after every round, a season-end screen and a history of your last seasons.
- **Instant replay**: the last 30 seconds of the race for every car are recorded (30 Hz ring buffer) and can be replayed from the pause menu or the results screen with TV / chase / low / helicopter / cockpit / T-cam cameras, slow motion, scrubbing and a choice of which car to follow.
- **Photo mode** (pause menu): free-fly camera with mouse look, zoom and a one-click PNG capture.

### Racing, rules and AI
- **AI recalibrated against human lap times** (Hautes-Fagnes 1:19, Vettore 1:08.5, Rade 1:06.4): brakes late and hard instead of bleeding speed over a long distance, speed envelope accounts for lateral load transfer, the racing line is smoothed so it can be driven at 340 km/h, longer steering preview. Legend: Vettore 1:08.0, Rade 1:05.3, Hautes-Fagnes 1:17.8, Northfield 1:22.3. Elite about 3 % slower, Pro 7 %, Amateur 13 %, Rookie 20 %.
- **Racecraft**: drivers now draft behind the car in front and pull out to pass, choose the inside of the next corner, defend once with a single move to cover the attacker's side (never weaving, never under braking), refuse to move into a gap another car is taking, hold their line when a car is alongside, and stay on the track. Following cars brake early enough to stop behind a slower or braking car, take corners a little slower in dirty air, and drive to what a damaged or punctured car has left.
- **Identical cars** option (default on): every team has exactly the player's car; only the drivers differ. Off keeps the team pecking order (career always uses it).
- **Safety car**: three or more stranded cars deploy the pace car (yellow flag first for a single stranded car). The field queues behind it, DRS and boost are off, overtaking behind it earns the player a 5 s penalty, cars with worn tyres pit cheaply, and it comes in when the track has been clear for a while, followed by a green flag.
- **Weather**: Dry, Light rain, Heavy rain or Changeable (a dry start with a shower). Rain cuts slick-tyre grip by about 30 % and makes kerbs slippery; the new Wet tyre gives the grip back (and runs cool, but wears and overheats on a dry track). Tyres cool faster on a wet track. AI cars start on and switch to the right tyres, brake earlier and are less aggressive in the wet. Radio calls for the forecast, a wet track and a drying track.
- **Time of day**: midday to dusk over the race (sky, sun colour and angle, longer shadows, exposure), or a full night race with stars, floodlight masts, pools of light on the road and the player's headlights.
- **Interactive pit stops**: press the DRS key as each of the four wheel-nut markers enters the green. A flawless stop is 2.5 s, a so-so press 0.12 s, a cross-threaded nut costs 0.9 s (toggle in Assists).
- **Damage model**: sidepods are separate structures that crumple on side impacts (visible deformation), front and rear wings bend and break, floor damage reduces downforce. The HUD shows wing / sidepod / rear wing health and the downforce lost, heavy damage sets a pit request automatically (B cancels), and repairs add time to the stop in proportion to the damage. AI pit for any severe damage.
- **Tyres**: a lock-up now flat-spots the tyre, spikes its temperature (capped) and accelerates its wear; flat spots cost grip until the tyres are changed.
- **Stewards**: running into the back of the car ahead at speed is a "causing a collision" penalty (5 s, then 10 s, then 20 s for the player; 5 s for AI).
- **DRS** verified end to end: only inside a zone, only after crossing the detection line within 1.0 s of the car ahead, never on lap 1, in the pit lane, under the safety car or in a corner. Painted detection and activation lines and DRS / DRS DETECTION / DRS END signs at every zone.
- **Slipstream** verified: about +15 % extra acceleration 12 m behind and +10 % at 20 m at 270 km/h.
- In-race tuning keys: **X** ERS mode (harvest / balanced / attack), **Z** fuel mix (lean / standard / rich), **[ ]** live brake bias.
- **Pit-entry fixes (changeable weather)**: cars now slow to the pit limit *before* the entry ramp starts turning (they used to brake and steer at the same time and spin on the ramp), call the pits for wet tyres as soon as rain arrives rather than once the track is soaked, abandon a missed entry and try again next lap, and a car wedged in the pit lane is recovered by the marshals like any other. A changeable-weather race went from about 80 marshal recoveries to about 20; the whole field finishes.
- Safety car: a car heading for the pits now obeys the pace-car speed cap until it reaches the pit lane.
- **Pit-wall strategy**: the race setup has a Pit wall option (Silent / Radio advice / Calls you in). The engineers read your actual tyre wear, project it to the flag, and call the lap to box on and the tyre to fit (wets when the track is soaked, slicks when it dries, the mandatory stop before the last laps). The tyre panel shows the box lap, and in the last two laps nothing is called unless the weather demands it. `tests/strategy.mjs` covers it.
- **Tyre pressure setup** (Team & car tab, -3..+3): higher pressure heats the tyres faster and wears them more slowly, lower pressure grips better in the wet; the standard setting is the dry sweet spot. **Live telemetry** (key Y): the last twelve seconds of speed, throttle, brake and steering as traces on screen.
- **Brake discs**: each wheel now has a disc temperature (heated by braking power, cooled by speed). Cold carbon bites less, above about 880 C it fades (up to 30 % less torque), and the discs glow orange to white through the wheels from 450 C. The tyre panel shows the hottest disc and the radio warns before fade; street circuits (Rade, Porto Vecchio) cook the brakes most. Typical peaks are 800-1150 C; lap times are unchanged.
- **Career forecasts**: every round of a career season has a fixed forecast (dry, showers possible, light or heavy rain), shown on the career hub; wet-weather rounds start the player on wets.
- **Fuel is burnt per distance, not per second**. A slow driver, a car waiting for the marshals or one stuck in a queue used to run the tank dry before the flag (a 5-lap race at slow pace ran out of fuel and cars stopped on hills). The tank loaded for the race distance now always lasts, and a field of 20 at Rookie level went from 105 marshal recoveries to none.
- **Longer pit-entry and exit ramps**: the sideways move into the pit lane is now spread over a distance that keeps the lateral load under about 0.75 g at the pit limit (about 85 m instead of 45-60 m), so cars no longer spin at the entry, and the lane geometry on every circuit follows.
- **AI tyre strategy**: starting compounds are chosen so the first stint covers at least 45 % of the race; stops are called from the wear actually measured over the last completed lap (the first lap on a set counts for 80 %, launch and warm-up included); the next set is the softest one that lasts to the flag at the chosen wear setting; the old wait-for-the-cliff rule is a last resort. A 20-car Legend race went from about 150 recoveries to under 12.
- The AI now brakes for the discs it actually has (cold carbon bites less, cooked carbon fades), and chooses starting compounds that last about half the race with a margin; wall impacts in a 20-car Legend race fell from 30-50 to under 10.
- Top-level AI keep about 2 % in hand in a race compared with a hot lap (qualifying and time trial are unchanged).
- Track-limit and pit rules unchanged from 1.1; the fast-forward to results is now about 20 times faster.

### Robustness
- Ninth review round (HUD, track): a new session starts with a clean HUD (no leftover finish banner, confetti, start lights or telemetry trace); the pit-stop panel reports the real stop time; overlapping HUD timers no longer hide a fresh key hint or the start lights; driver and team names are escaped in the finish banner; the pit panel only rebuilds when its text changes; track generation cannot loop forever when a kerb or run-off range ends on the last sample.
- Realism pass, graphics and trackside: the road is now shaded per pixel. After rain the **racing line dries first** (patchily, with puddles and reflections staying off line) and the dry line is real: a car on it has more grip than one off it. **Rubber builds up** on the line over a race (darker, with a sheen, and up to 1.2 % more grip in the dry). Asphalt and kerbs are **bump-mapped**, so low sun and floodlights pick out the grain and the saw-tooth kerb ridges. Tyres have rounded shoulders instead of being plain cylinders. Cars gain bargeboards, floor strakes and edges, diffuser fences, front-wing cascades and dive planes, and a **steering wheel with a live display** (shift lights, gear, speed, battery, DRS, position) that turns with your steering in the cockpit and T-cam views. Trackside: a **bridge over the track at each sector line**, braking boards on **both** sides of the road, and **marshal posts** every 350 m whose flags wave yellow under a caution or safety car, green at the start and chequered at the finish. Post-processing adds **heat haze** behind the exhausts of the nearest four cars and a soft **sun glare** with a streak when the low sun is in shot (hidden when something is in front of it).
- Realism pass, the car on the road: **live suspension** - each corner's spring travel follows its real vertical load, so the body squats under power, dives under braking, leans in corners, sits down at speed and the wheels work over kerbs while the wishbones swing to follow; wheels stay on the road instead of pitching with the body. **Crests and compressions** are in the physics: the car goes light over a crest and is pressed into the road in a dip (Sakura runs from 0.70 g to 1.35 g), the speed profile and the rivals' braking know about it, and gravity along a slope lengthens or shortens braking. **Floor bottoming**: at very high speed or in a fast compression the plank touches down, with a shower of sparks from under the rear of the car and a scrape you can hear.
- Realism pass, tyres: each tyre now has a fast **surface** temperature on top of the slow **carcass** temperature. The surface flashes 15-20 K above the carcass in a hard corner and 100 K in a lock-up, and is cooled by the airflow on the straights; the carcass still sets the grip window and the degradation, so lap times are unchanged (within 0.05 s on four circuits). A cooked surface costs grip at once. **Graining**: pushing on cold rubber tears the surface and costs up to 7 % grip until warm laps clean it up - most likely on the lap after a pit stop. The HUD tyre colours follow the surface and it warns when the tyres are graining. The outside tyres of a lap wear measurably faster (Northfield: 28.4 % against 25.3 %). `tests/tyres.mjs`.
- Realism pass, rivals: **multi-lap energy plan** - a rival stuck behind a car of the same pace stops wasting its battery, harvests for a lap or two, then spends all of it down one long straight with DRS and the tow. **Undercut and overcut**: inside its pit window a rival stuck within 1.5 s of the car ahead stops early for clean air, and one whose rival has just pitted stays out a lap longer if its tyres have life (one car at a time, never into a busy pit lane - unrationed, 11 cars tried it on the same lap). **Switchback**: an attacker boxed out on the outside at the turn-in slows in and cuts back under the rival for the exit instead of lifting. 20-car race at F1 level: Rade 77 passes with no contact; Northfield 103 passes, 7 car contacts.
- Realism pass, sound: a **gearbox whine** pitched by shaft speed and loudest under load, a **turbo whistle** that spools with boost, **wastegate flutter** when you lift off at full boost, and the floor scraping.
- Measured and not adopted: an asymmetric (late-apex) racing line. Two versions were tried - sliding slow-corner apexes 4-12 m later, and weighting the solver to straighten exits at the cost of entries. The mildest exit-weighting was level with the symmetric line and everything stronger was slower, on six circuits (by up to 1.3 s a lap), so the symmetric line stays; the exit-weighting remains in the solver as a switched-off tunable (`LINE_TUNE` in `src/sim/track.js`).
- A better racing line. The old line was a minimum-curvature solution smoothed so heavily (24 m) and tied so strongly to path length that it hardly used the width of the track. It is now a full-width, late-apex line (path-length weight 0.001, 10 m smoothing), and its quality was measured, not assumed: the AI drives each candidate line in the real physics simulation. Result on all 12 circuits: laps 7-12 % faster with no track-limit violations (Rade 65.4 s to 59.3 s). Because a faster line is no use if you cannot stay on the road, it is kept 1.9 m inside the edge (2.6 m on street circuits, where it also stays 3.2 m from the barrier), and rivals keep their planned passing positions 2.0 m (2.8 m between walls) from the edge; at that setting the 20-car field races cleanly on Rade, Porto and Lumiere. The guide ribbon starts under your car and eases onto the line over 55 m (it used to jump to the line), widens with distance so it stays readable, and has a white bar at each braking point and a square at each apex. Rivals ride the same line, so every difficulty level is about 9 % quicker than before (README pace table regenerated); to keep the straights honest the DRS / straight check now tolerates the line's gentle lateral sweep. Solver parameters can be tuned with `computeRacingLine(track, margin, lengthWeight, smoothLen)`.
- Career rules: a career now has editable rules, on the New Career screen and, at any time, from the hub's *Career rules* button. Race rules: **laps per race** (0 follows the distance preset, 1-30 fixes it, so 1 makes every round a one-lap sprint), cars on the grid (2-20), your start position without qualifying, weather (each round's forecast, or always dry / light rain / heavy rain / changeable), tyre wear (off, low, normal, high), pit-wall help, damage, collisions, a mandatory stop and adaptive AI pace. Season rules: **rounds per season** (1-24; a long season goes round the circuits again), race distance, AI difficulty (now including F1, and it stays where you set it), whether the rivals improve every season, and a **prize-money multiplier** (x0.5 to x5) for race prizes and the season bonus. Changes apply from the next race (rounds from the next season); old saves get the defaults, and an old '8 rounds' season keeps meaning every circuit. `tests/careerrules.mjs` covers the rules, the prize multiplier, the F1 level surviving a new season, old saves and a real one-lap race on two circuits.
- Rival racecraft, a full decision matrix (`src/sim/racecraft.js`): every rival now thinks ten times a second. Perception turns the traffic into ten 0..1 features (how tightly it sits behind the car ahead, its pace edge, braking zone, corner proximity, straight, room beside the target, DRS, a threat behind, a lapping leader, risk from rain, damage and penalties). Seven actions each have a row of weights in a matrix - LINE, FOLLOW, DRAFT, ATTACK, COVER, BLOCK, YIELD - scaled by the driver's own aggression / defence / risk-tolerance; impossible actions are masked, the current one gets a hysteresis bonus, the best wins. What they do with it: they sit in your tow on the straights, tuck onto the inside of the next corner, dive at the braking zone with a later brake point (up to 11 % more brake demand for a committed attack), abandon a dive that has not got its nose alongside by the turn-in, cover your line on a straight with one move, take the inside before the braking zone to close the door, and pull aside for a lapping leader with a lift. A human rival gets more room alongside than an AI car. Low-level drivers (novices) never defend. `RaceBrain.explain()` returns every score, and with the live telemetry open (Y) the HUD shows the matrix of the car ahead and the car behind you. `tests/brain.mjs` pins the decisions for 20 situations.
- Rivals use the car properly: they run their own ERS mode (attack mode in a fight, harvest while stuck in a car's wake, a last-laps flat-out deploy), their own fuel mix (lean when short, rich in a fight) from a measured fuel burn per lap, open the ERS boost when attacking or defending, and on the straights they no longer stop at the plain profile speed: with the DRS flap open or the boost on they keep accelerating (+5 % / +2 % target speed), like the car physically can. Traffic awareness reaches 190 m ahead and scales with the closing speed, so a car crawling into the pit lane at 300 km/h of closing speed is seen in time. A new **F1** difficulty (level 1.15: flawless, relentless, near-zero mistakes) tops the presets. `tools/racing-quality.mjs` counts passes, contacts, recoveries and penalties for a 20-car race: against the old line-followers, wall hits fell from 112 to 55, recoveries from 43 to 32 and car contacts from 56 to 47 on three circuits.
- Rivals judge traffic by where it is going, not only where it is: each car tracks the lateral speed of its neighbours, a car merging onto the line (a pit exit at 80 km/h with 300 km/h of closing speed, a defender moving across) counts as a hazard before it arrives (a wider reach for pit-exit cars), a dive is dropped when the car ahead has moved across and closed the gap, and nobody tucks in behind a crawling car. Race punctures are now deterministic so a replayed race is the same race. With these, the 20-car field at F1 level has 2-3 car contacts and no wall hits or recoveries on Rade and Northfield (`tools/racing-quality.mjs`).
- The blanket speed-up for every car was taken back out (it only made the same race faster): the stock car again launches 0-100 km/h in about 2.6 s and every car, rivals included, is identical (`CAR_BASE` is neutral). *Standard* is the default for your car again; *Quick* (+8 % power, firmer launch) and *Rocket* (+30 %) stay available as a handicap for starting at the back. The field gets faster through smarter, more precise driving instead (next entries).
- Car feel and graphics round: (1) the G-force meter now shows the force you feel, the way racing sims do: braking throws the dot forward (up), accelerating pushes it back, and a left turn throws it right (it was drawn the opposite way on both axes). (2) A new *Engine & grip* choice on the Team & car tab boosts your car only: **Quick** (+8 % power, +3 % downforce and grip, extra rear traction off the line) and **Rocket** (+30 % power) are optional boosts; **Standard** (default) is the identical car every rival drives. A bot driving at the field's pace from the back of the grid gains 4 places on the first lap with Quick (P20 to P16 after 20 s) and finishes P7, where the Standard car stays stuck in the train until the pit stops. Not applied in Career. `tests/carpower.mjs`. (3) Graphics: a colour grade (a little more contrast and colour, cool shadows) with a soft vignette on Medium and High, a soft contact shadow under every car on every quality level, and a one-sample fast path so the grade costs one cheap full-screen pass. The verges beside the track now show mown stripes (bands of cut grass along the lap).
- Two new circuits, twelve in all: *Circuit de la Bastide* (3.6 km medieval harbour town, street walls, high downforce) and *Caldera Speedway* (5.2 km fast open bowl around a volcano, 34 m of elevation). Both pass the track sanity check and the full-grid matrix at three difficulty levels; Legend laps 1:14.3 and 1:13.5. The README pace table was regenerated for all twelve; the all-circuits career calendar now has twelve rounds.
- Ghost sharing: the time-trial setup (Session tab) has a Ghost panel. *Copy my ghost* gives one line of text (`AGP-GHOST|circuit|lap time|frames`) for the circuit you have selected; paste a friend's line and *Use pasted ghost* makes it the ghost to chase on that circuit. A pasted ghost is verified before it is saved: real circuit, plausible lap time, finite coordinates, and a recording length that matches the lap time. `tests/ghost.mjs`.
- The sponsor lettering on the right-hand barrier read backwards (AXIOM as MOIXA) because the texture ran along the lap the same way on both walls; it is now mirrored for that side so every board reads correctly from the track. `tests/shot21.mjs` takes in-race frames for any circuit, weather and time of day.
- Twentieth review round (track): a circuit definition without a usable start position now fails loudly instead of building a track full of NaN; the start position is wrapped into range; a pit lane defined across the start line keeps its authored length; two no-op expressions and a stale DRS comment fixed. Reviewed and left alone on purpose: the racing-line solver's active-set loop runs all 60 iterations rather than converging (it costs under 130 ms per track and the AI's calibrated lap times depend on the line it produces), and the pit-surface test near the ramp start.
- Nineteenth review round (menus): the two-click 'Clear saved records' confirmation no longer stays armed after the screen changes (a single later click could delete everything); settings no longer rewrite the whole save and resize the renderer on every slider event (the save is written once it settles, the resize only when quality or window size changed); the showroom car is rebuilt when the team changes, not on every tab or chip click; the career footer shows the real round count for 'all circuits'; a stored season keeps its forecast aligned with the calendar; the number validator no longer treats `true`, `[]` or blank text as numbers (a foreign backup could set full volume); Quick race ignores a stale 'qualify first' flag; Helicopter is a selectable default camera; the driver-code field shows the kept value when you stop typing.
