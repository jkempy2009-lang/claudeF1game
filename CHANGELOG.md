# Changelog

## 1.2 - the big overnight update

Everything below was added or fixed in this release, grouped by area.

### Latest: tyres last a set number of laps
- **Fixed: tyres wore out in under a lap in short races.** Tyre wear had been scaled to the race distance (each compound lasting the share of the race it does in a 56-lap Grand Prix), with no lower limit: a 1-lap race wore 56 times faster than real and a Soft was gone in a third of a lap, a 3-lap race 19 times faster. Now each compound lasts a set number of laps whatever the race length. Measured at the limit on Realistic: a Soft reaches the grip cliff after about 17 laps (Northfield 17, Rade 18), a Medium about 30 (28, 31), a Hard about 45 (41, 50); a third fewer on the stop-start Kasbah Streets (11 / 18 / 26). Low makes them last twice as long, High half as long. The settings say so. `tests/tyres.mjs` checks the wear rate no longer depends on the race length.

### Latest: work from six outside models, checked, repaired and integrated
Six other AI models were each given one self-contained job from a written brief, without access to the code; their answers were pasted in and integrated here. What each delivered, and what had to change:
- **Five new circuits** (from 6.2 Sol): *Neon Crescent* (5.3 km, dusk-lit city streets, walls half a metre away), *Meridian Airfield* (5.3 km, a 2 km runway, the fastest straight of all), *Azure Scarp* (5.3 km, cliff-top coast road with a 30 m plunge at 11 %), *Ironwood Ascent* (4.6 km, pine forest, 47 m of height), *Aurora Frost Circuit* (4.4 km, snow and a frozen lake). Repairs: two layouts (Ironwood, Frost) doubled back on themselves into the start and would not build, so their last sector was rerouted; every corner had been drawn with a 20-40 m radius, even 20° kinks, so all five drove like strings of 110-140 km/h corners and lap averages were alike: the shallow kinks are now fast sweepers (150-300 m), the big corners after the straights stay slow for overtaking; the start straights of Neon and Scarp were lengthened; the descriptions were rewritten to match what was built (the drafts promised a rock tunnel and ice run-off that are not there); Frost got snow. The model's own validator was wrong (it measured the inside angle of each corner instead of the turn), which is why it passed layouts that crash. Checked: every constraint by the track builder; the AI laps each cleanly; 20-car races over 2 seeds each finish all cars with 0-1 wall hits and 3-6 contacts (Kasbah: 0 and 3); the rival pace table is regenerated (20 circuits).
- **Career story** (from a 5.6 Sol, in Fable 5.2's role): after every race the press ask a question that fits what happened (a win, a DNF, a penalty, beating or losing to the team-mate, a wet race, a contract year, a title fight...), with a humble, confident, combative and diplomatic answer to choose in the hub. Answers move reputation, the garage's morale (worth up to +/-2 budget a race) and the relationship with the team-mate; confident and combative ones can promise a result (points, a podium, a win, beating the team-mate) within three rounds, and the press remember: kept, the paddock rates you higher; broken, it costs more than it gained. Rivalries build from contact and positions swapped on track, a newspaper headline leads the paddock news, and the engineer has lines for the start, places won and lost (at most one every 30 s), the last lap, your fastest lap and the flag. Repairs: the draft gave the same four answers to every question ("It was a proper team result" after a DNF); every situation has its own answers now. Its promises were never checked; several questions could never be asked; headlines broke with a missing name. `tools/story-samples.mjs` (in the test suite) checks 30 seeded seasons of text.
- **V6-hybrid engine sound** (from Sonnet 5.5, in Opus 5.5's role), Settings > Engine sound: two detuned firing oscillators at the V6's firing rate, a soft-clipped mid-range rasp, turbo whistle and wastegate chatter on a lift, MGU-K whine with road speed, a 40 ms ignition cut on upshifts, blips and pops on downshifts, overrun crackle, the rev limiter and a stuttering pit limiter, a booming cockpit and a brighter exterior; the cars around you get a lighter voice with Doppler and air absorption. The answer was cut off in the middle of its update function, so the rest was written here to its design. Rendered offline: no clipping, no invalid samples, the limiter and overrun audibly distinct. Nobody has judged it by ear yet, so the original engine stays the default.
- **Tyre and pit sounds** (from Grok 4.7): a tyre voice per wheel, panned, so you hear which end is sliding; a lock-up is a higher, narrower shriek than a cornering squeal, wheelspin a lower chirp; nothing on grass or gravel, a hiss instead of a squeal in the wet. The pit lane has its own hum; a stop has the air jacks and four wheel guns (one per button press in the interactive stop); gravel sprays and carbon fragments skitter after a hit. Repairs: the draft re-created eight layers the game already had, drove kerb and gravel sounds from tyre slip instead of the surface, squealed on grass, filtered brown noise where it has no energy, played 5-10x louder than the mix and never stopped its tyre sources; only the new parts were kept, levels matched to the old squeal by offline rendering. Its team-radio filter is not used: the engineer speaks through the browser's speech synthesis, which Web Audio cannot process.
- **Sky** (from 6.1 Terra): not used as a whole. It was a fixed gradient rather than the scattering model asked for, ignored each circuit's own sky colours (a 12 % tint) and did its own tone mapping, so night exposure would have broken; the game's sky already does per-circuit colour, dusk, night, rain and lit clouds. Two ideas were taken into it: lightning in heavy rain (the cloud deck flashes, the ambient light flickers with it) and dithering against colour banding at dusk.
- **Brakes, found while checking the new circuits.** Measured over two flying laps on every circuit: the discs peaked at 1170-1300 C at Kasbah, Bastide, Neon, Sakura and the Serpentine, and the brakes started to fade at 960 C, so ordinary heavy stops faded them; at Kasbah the reliability model would have failed the brake line within about five laps. Carbon brakes now fade from 1050 C (F1 discs reach about 1000 C at the heaviest stops of the season without trouble), brake wear starts at 1080 C, and each circuit can size its brake ducts as teams do (bigger at those five). Now every circuit peaks between 930 and 1110 C with no brake wear, lap times within 0.3 s. The AI's braking estimate used the old fade figures (880 C); it now uses the same ones as the car, and so do the HUD's FADE tag and the engineer's warning.
- **Trackside scenery kit** (from ChatGPT Astra 6.2): not used. Every prop already exists in the game, and the kit was far over its budgets (one draw call per seat: about 1,300 for one grandstand against a budget of 4; one per tyre in a tyre wall), and two builders would have crashed on first use.

### Latest: a transfer market that moves
Measured with the new `tools/market-sim.mjs` (60 simulated winters with plausible results): the old market moved about 4 AI drivers a winter (two swaps, at most three a winter by rule), and a player got 0.6 offers a winter, only ever from faster teams, so the driver of the best car could never move at all.
- **Every AI driver has a contract** (1-3 seasons, shown next to his name in the market tab). When it runs out his team keeps him only if he still meets what it expects, and not always then; a driver in poor form can be dropped under contract; one in good form can be bought out by a faster team.
- **Seats are filled from the best car down.** Each team with a seat open takes the best driver it can get (a free agent, a driver it buys out from a slower team, or a rookie, though the front-running teams rarely gamble on one), and every driver who moves up leaves a seat behind, so one move sets off the next. A driver who finds no seat waits a year, then retires. Now: about 5 AI drivers change team every winter, plus 2 retirements and 2 rookies, roughly nine changes to the grid.
- **Mid-season changes.** At the halfway point a team may replace the driver who is furthest behind his team-mate (at least 8 points), with the best driver without a seat or a rookie: about one change every other season.
- **Offers from teams below you too.** Every team whose standard you meet makes an offer: up to three faster cars, and at least one team further down, who wants a driver above its level and pays for him (longer contracts, up to 1.6x). The best car's driver now has a choice every winter. The reputation a team asks for is lower (78 for the best car, 7 less for each place down, was 85 and 8). Teams at your level and below show interest at mid-season as well. Offers say whether they are a step up or down. Now: 3-4 offers every winter whatever the results.

### Latest: tight street circuits, barriers without ledges, three new circuits
- **Barriers without ledges.** Every barrier line used to step sideways (1-4 m at once) wherever a kerb or a run-off area started or ended, and a car sliding along a wall caught on the steps. Each barrier is now continuous: it may step outward at once, but comes back in at no more than 1 m in 6, so there is nothing to catch on in either direction, and it never stands closer than designed. Cars also meet walls at eight points (the corners and the middle of each side), so a barrier that bulges towards the car, on the inside of a corner, can no longer slip between the corners. Physics and scenery use the same barrier line.
- **Street circuits made tight.** The walls at Rade, Parc Lumière and Porto Vecchio now stand 0.5-0.6 m from the track (were 1.4-1.8 m), at Bastide 0.35 m. The AI's racing line may run 1.8 m (centre to wall) from a street barrier instead of 3.2 m, as drivers do at Monaco.
- **An AI fault the tight walls exposed.** A car holding a big sideways offset from the racing line was carried with the line where it swings across the road before a corner, and the steering followed the swing instead of correcting the drift: at Parc Lumière every car in the opening-lap queue clipped the inside wall at the first corner (27 touches in one race). The AI now has eyes for the barrier: where will the car be in 0.6 s? Closer than 1.3 m (centre to wall) and it steers away (`RC_TUNE.wallEyes`); its planned path is kept off the barriers too (`RC_TUNE.pathWalls`). Over 4 dry races: Parc Lumière 76 wall touches with the tight walls before the fix, 4 after (4 with the old, wider walls); Bastide 51 → 0; Rade 22 → 4.
- **The AI in the rain, round two.** Teams now pit a car whose tyres are 68 % worn (it waited for 80 %, deep past the cliff at 62 %), and a spun car is only turned round by the marshals when it is really stuck (facing the wrong way below about 30 km/h for 3 s): on open run-off a spin sorts itself out, and the rescue had been counting ordinary spins as recoveries. Over 4 races: Caldera in light rain 63 recoveries → 16, Northfield in light rain 40 → 21, Sakura dry 26 → 8.
- **Three new circuits** that go up and down and side to side. *Serpentine Pass* (5.6 km): a mountain road made into a circuit, a 73 m climb through switchback esses with ten changes of direction, a blind crest and a plunging descent, guard rails on both sides. *Vortex Ring* (4.9 km): banked bowls you can lean on (12°), crests down both straights that make the car light, a corkscrew that drops off a crest. *Kasbah Streets* (4.3 km): the narrowest circuit, 10 m wide with walls 0.25 m from the track, right-angle corners and 10 % grades through the old town. Circuits can now place single crests and dips (`hills`) and bank every corner of a given radius (`bankCorners`).
- **Before and after, over the whole calendar.** 4 races per circuit and condition at level 0.8, against the code from before today: dry wall hits Vettore 23 → 0, Northfield 25 → 10, Costa 18 → 2, Aurora 17 → 2, Mirage 19 → 10; dry contacts down at 9 of the 12 old circuits. The rival pace table is regenerated for all 15 circuits. `tests/walls.mjs` checks the barriers, the street walls, the new circuits' shape and that the AI laps each of them cleanly.

### Latest: smarter bots in the wet; tyres and penalties calibrated to real F1
- **Why the bots spun in the rain, and the fix.** Traced car by car, the crashes that were left at Bastide and Sakura in the wet had nothing to do with corner speed: braking for a slow corner, the automatic gearbox went down to first and the engine braking locked the rear wheels on the soaked track. The car has engine-braking control now (`PHYS.ebControl`): the overrun torque eases off when the rears start to lock, as real F1 engine-braking maps do. A car left facing the wrong way at low speed after a spin is turned round and sent off within about 2 s (`RC_TUNE.wrongWay`) instead of crawling the wrong way into the next wall. Light rain over 4 races: Bastide 158 wall hits / 91 recoveries / 60 contacts before, 5 / 7 / 6 after (dry Bastide: 4 / 6 / 21); Sakura 61 / 51 / 38 before, 7 / 35 / 29 after. Gentler throttle at low speed in the wet was tried first and made things much worse (Sakura 351 wall hits): it was taken out.
- **Penalties per the F1 guidelines (2025, and the 2026 version).** Jump start 5 s (10 s if the car crept more than 2 m). Causing a collision 10 s, 5 s when mitigated (the opening lap, a light touch); no more 5 / 10 / 20 escalation. Pit-lane speeding by the excess: up to 6 km/h over 5 s, 6-15 km/h a drive-through, more a 10 s stop-go; the game cannot serve those in the pit lane, so they are added as F1 does in the last laps, 20 s and 30 s. Overtaking under the safety car or the VSC: 10 s, nothing if the place is given back within 15 s (the HUD and the engineer tell you). VSC delta: 5 s, 10 s when more than 3 s under the minimum time. Track limits (still only when time was gained): each strike deletes the lap, the third brings the black-and-white flag, the fourth and every one after it 5 s.
- **Tyres calibrated against F1.** Measured with `tools/tyre-stints.mjs` (one car at the limit, real wear) at Northfield: Soft used up in 18 laps losing 0.12 s a lap, Medium 30 laps and 0.06 s a lap, Hard 45 laps (slow to warm, then steady); Soft to Medium 0.57 s, Medium to Hard 0.50 s (F1: about 15-25 / 25-35 / 40-50 laps, 0.3-0.6 s a step). Before: 9 / 16 / 24 laps, 0.26-1.0 s a lap of degradation and 0.63 s steps. The degradation was mostly heat, not wear: the tyres settle at about 105 C in a race on every kind of circuit, and the working windows sat below that, in the wrong order (the Hard coolest). They are centred on it now, harder compounds hotter, as Pirelli's are. Tyres start a race 15 C under their window (the 0-100 km/h launch stays at 2.6 s).
- **Tyre wear is scaled to the race distance** (replaced later: tyres now last a set number of laps, see above). *Realistic* (was *Normal*) makes each compound last the share of the race it does in a real Grand Prix, whatever the number of laps: a Soft about a third, a Medium about a half, a Hard about three quarters, so most drivers stop once. Low halves the wear, High doubles it. The AI's tyre-life estimate and its choice of starting tyre follow the measured wear.

### Latest: the AI in the rain, track limits judged on advantage, retiring from a race
- **AI in the rain.** The AI's grip estimate left out what the physics takes away in the wet besides the tyre's own wet grip: the greasy rubbered line, the pressure window moving lower. It drove a few percent past the real limit and found the walls. It now counts both and keeps a margin in the wet (6 %, reached at a damp track on street circuits with walls at the edge, at a wet one elsewhere), each behind its own switch in `RC_TUNE` (`wetGrip`, `wetMargin`). Over 4 races each at level 0.8: Rade in heavy rain 73 wall hits / 25 recoveries / 27 contacts before, 1 / 1 / 2 after; Rade in light rain 25 / 22 / 19 → 4 / 6 / 4; Northfield in heavy rain 43 / 21 / 26 → 18 / 10 / 22; Bastide in light rain 391 / 209 / 141 → 158 / 91 / 60 (still the worst place to race in the rain). Wet laps are 11-14 % slower than dry. `tools/racing-quality.mjs` and `tools/incident-causes.mjs` take `WEATHER=light|heavy`.
- **Track limits judged on advantage.** A trip off the track used to earn a warning every 2.5 s while the car was out there, and three warnings were a penalty, gained or not. Now one excursion is one incident, judged when the car is back on the road against its own best time through that stretch: no gain, nothing at all (the lap stands); 0.1-0.4 s gained, a warning and the lap time deleted (three warnings, 5 s); more than 0.4 s, or a place taken off the track and not given back within about 5 s, a 5 s penalty. Qualifying and time trials still delete any lap that leaves the track.
- **Retire from the race.** The pause menu has *Retire from the race* in any race (two clicks): you are classified as a DNF, "Retired by the driver", and the results come up straight away. In a career it counts like any non-finish.

### Latest: sprint weekends fixed for short races, and a switch on the hub
- **A sprint is now always shorter than its race.** It was a third of the distance but at least 2 laps, so a career set to 1-lap races got a 2-lap sprint before a 1-lap Grand Prix. Now it is a third of the distance, at least 1 lap, and sprint weekends only happen when the Grand Prix is 3 laps or more (3-4 laps: a 1-lap sprint; 6: 2; 15: 5). On a round that would have been a sprint weekend but whose race is too short, the hub says so.
- **Switch sprints off where they are announced.** The race-weekend tab now has a *Sprint weekends: on / off* button next to the sprint notice (the same setting as in Career rules).

### Latest: formation lap and career practice (SPEC-v2 section 9, the last items)
- **Formation lap** (a new *Session* option, off by default; it also applies to career races). At the start the field pulls away in grid order and drives a slow lap: the front at about 110 km/h, everyone else closing to ten metres behind the car ahead. Each car weaves on the straights and now and then drops back, then sprints and brakes hard to close up again. When the front of the field is back at the grid, every car is put in its own grid box (the lining-up is a cut, not driven) with the heat it built up, and the lights come on. No lap is counted, and no timing, rules or pit calls run during the lap. Measured over 10 and 20 cars at three circuits: front brakes reach the grid at 330-490 °C (they leave at 260 °C); the tyres keep their blanket heat (85 → 83 °C); no contact. `tests/formation.mjs` checks all of this.
- **Career practice.** Before a weekend's first session the hub offers a practice: alone on the circuit, with a run plan. A qualifying simulation (one clean lap within 2 % of the rivals' pace at your difficulty) earns +0.6 % grip for the weekend; a long run (four clean laps in a row within 1.5 % of their average) earns -10 % tyre wear. *End practice* in the pause menu goes back to the hub with what was earned. Practice laps do not set records or ghosts.

### Latest: spectator mode, podium, broadcast graphics, weather radar, worn tyres, colour-blind HUD (SPEC-v2 section 9)
- **Watch a race.** A new main-menu entry starts a race with every car on AI (your seat goes to a reserve driver, "RES", on the standard car and setup). A TV director cuts every nine seconds to the closest fight on track, with a "Battle for P4" banner, and changes between the TV, helicopter, chase and T-cam angles. The player's own instruments are hidden. Nothing is saved: no records, stats, achievements or championship points.
- **Podium.** When a race is left to run out (the finish card times out) the top three are parked side by side past the line for about eight seconds with a slow camera arc and their names on the steps. Enter, Space, Esc or a click skips it; skipping the finish card goes straight to the results as before.
- **Broadcast graphics.** The timing tower shows how many laps each car's tyres have done. When a rival is stationary in its pit box, a "PIT STOP" clock times it live at the top of the screen (the highest-placed one if several stop together).
- **Weather radar.** In a race with rain in the forecast, the top bar shows one cell per lap for the next eight laps, coloured by how hard it will rain, from the race's own weather timeline.
- **Worn tyres.** Close-up cars' tyres turn from glossy black to a dusty grey as they wear, with streaks across the tread as they grain and dark specks of picked-up rubber on the shoulders.
- **Colour-blind HUD.** A new setting swaps the green / red pairs for blue / orange: sectors, tyre temperatures, damage, the delta bar, the pedal bars, the MFD and the replay markers.
- **Sprint weekends in the career.** In a season of four rounds or more, round 2 and every third round after it is a sprint weekend: a sprint of a third of the distance (at least 2 laps) with points for the top eight (8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1, no fastest-lap point), counted in both championships. Its finishing order is the Grand Prix grid. The hub offers *Qualify & sprint* (qualifying sets the sprint grid) or *Sprint now*, then *Grand Prix*. A new career rule turns sprint weekends off. Not done from the spec item: practice sessions with a run plan and setup bonuses.

### Latest: sound, HUD, replay and photo mode (SPEC-v2 sections 4 and 8)
- **Engine firing pulses (8.1).** The engine note is modulated at the firing frequency of a V6 (revs ÷ 60 × 3), and lifting off at high revs brings in a resonant overrun band (180-320 Hz, falling with the revs), so a lift sounds like an engine on the overrun rather than a filter closing.
- **What the tyres are on (8.1).** Kerbs thump at 35-75 Hz (rising with speed; the spec's sync to the kerb stripes is not done), gravel crackles (high-passed noise at 1.8 kHz), grass is a muffled brush (400 Hz). The old shared rumble is halved.
- **Buffeting, squeal, scrape (4.1).** Running into or out of another car's wake buffets at 95 Hz. Carbon brakes squeal (3150 + 4720 Hz) when you brake gently at low speed with cool discs. The plank scrape now has a ring-modulated metallic band (2.4 / 4.8 kHz) on top.
- **Radio (8.1, 9).** Each radio message opens a crackle of static for its length, and UI tones go through the radio band. New *Race engineer voice* setting (on by default): the engineer speaks the messages with the browser's speech synthesis. The voice is not routed through the radio filter: browsers do not let a page process speech-synthesis audio.
- **Damage diagram (4.2).** A top-down outline of the car beside the tyres; front wing, rear wing, floor and each sidepod turn from green through amber to red with their damage.
- **Delta bar (4.2).** Under the lap timer, a centre-zero bar (160 × 6 px, ±1 s): green to the left when you are ahead of your best lap, red to the right when behind; during a VSC it shows the VSC delta instead.
- **Multi-function display (8.2).** Press **I** to show it and to page through: tyres and brakes (skin, core and brake temperatures and wear per corner, the compound's window), strategy and fuel (fuel left, laps of fuel in hand, mix and ERS mode, stops, the pit wall's call, power-unit health), sectors and damage (last and best splits, damage per area, downforce lost). A fourth press hides it. Rebindable on the controls screen.
- **Live setup numbers (8.2).** The car setup tab shows top speed, 0-100 km/h, cornering at 120 and 250 km/h and the aero balance for the current wings, with the change from the recommended setup, updated as you drag. They come from the same car model the AI uses for its speed profiles: right relative to each other, not lap-exact (the standard car comes out at 2.52 s to 100 km/h against 2.6 s on track).
- **Replay (8.3).** The instant replay keeps 60 seconds (was 30), at 30 Hz rather than the 20 Hz the spec suggested (about 2.4 MB for 20 cars). The scrub bar has markers for your position changes, contact, your pit stop, fastest laps, retirements, safety cars and penalties; clicking one jumps to 2.5 s before it and follows the car involved. The markers sit at the recorded frame nearest their race time, so they stay right if the game drops frames.
- **Photo mode (8.3).** Z / X roll the camera, − / + change the exposure (shown in EV), *Slow shutter* (B) turns on a long-exposure blur and *Level* resets roll and exposure. The exposure is put back when you leave. The blur is the game's radial speed blur, so the centre of the frame stays sharp rather than a tracked car.

### Latest: graphics and lighting (SPEC-v2 sections 2 and 6)
- **Road surface (2.1).** The asphalt has a normal map made from its own height texture (a Sobel filter), so the aggregate catches low sun. The rubbered line is a little glossier than the tarmac beside it, and the braking zones darken with the rubber laid there. The barriers get a normal map too, and the kerbs are scuffed where cars run over them.
- **Reflections that follow the sky (2.2).** The environment map is rendered again from the sky as dusk, night or rain come in, so car paint and wet road reflect the sky as it is now rather than the midday sky from the start of the race. In the rain the road gets moving ripple rings in its normal map, and the floodlight pools on a wet road are softer.
- **Visor rain and backfires (2.3).** In the cockpit camera, rain beads on the visor and streaks sideways in corners and back at speed. Cars spit a short flame from the exhaust on an upshift at high revs and on a sharp lift.
- **Driver arms and landmarks (2.4).** In the cockpit view the driver's arms (in the team's suit colour) reach the wheel and follow it as it turns. Each circuit gets one or two landmarks from a table (a Ferris wheel, a control tower, yachts in the harbour, a bridge arch), corner number boards, LED panels that show the flag in force (yellow, VSC, SC) and searchlights at night.
- **Shadows and contact shading (6.1).** The sun's shadow covers a wider area around the camera car (±95 m), and each tyre sits on a soft contact shadow, so cars no longer look as if they hover.
- **Marbles you can see (6.2).** The marbles from the physics are drawn on the road beside the line, updated every two seconds.
- **Particles and brake dust (6.3).** Smoke and spray fade out as they reach the camera instead of cutting through it. Hard braking puffs carbon dust from the front wheels.
- **Cameras (6.4).** The TV camera sways a little and leads the car with its zoom. During your pit stop the view cuts to a camera in the box and back after.
- **The crowd** gets louder and waves more on the last lap.
- **Carbon fibre.** Bare carbon parts have a twill weave in their shader.
- **A texture leak fixed.** Each race used to leave two GPU textures behind when it ended (the reflection map's render target was released but not the texture inside it; the same with the marble map), so a long session of quick races slowly filled video memory. `tests/leak.mjs` caught it once the reflections started refreshing during a race; it now holds steady.
- **Not done, or changed from the spec.** The spec asked for a blended second shadow cascade; there is one wider shadow map instead, and shadows can still end abruptly at its edge. The spec's AgX tone mapping was tried and washed the colours out, so the game stays on ACES. The backfire is a flame sprite, not a light that lights the road.

### Latest: the driver market, contracts and season goals
- **A living grid.** A career has its own copy of the 20 drivers, each with an age and a talent, and races use it. Every winter the drivers age a year: up to 26 they improve, from 33 they decline, and a good or bad season nudges the change. Drivers retire from 34 (always by 38), and rookies from the junior series take their seats. Up to three transfers happen each winter: the best performer of a weaker team takes the seat of a disappointing driver at a stronger one, and they swap. A new **Driver market** tab shows every team's line-up with ages and talent, the driver without a seat, and last winter's moves; the moves also make the paddock news.
- **Contracts.** You start on a two-year deal. Every offer now comes with terms: one to three seasons, and pay from ×0.8 to ×1.6 on the team's retainer, longer and better paid the more the team wants you. While a contract runs, staying is your choice. When it runs out, the team offers a renewal if it still wants you. Joining another team puts its displaced driver in your old seat.
- **Mid-season interest.** At the halfway point of the season, the stronger teams whose standard you meet (or nearly meet) say so. It shows in the Driver tab and the news, and makes them a little easier to impress at the end of the season.
- **Season goals.** Each season the team sets three: a constructors' position, beating your team-mate in the standings, and a points haul scaled to the car and the calendar. Each one met pays a bonus and adds reputation; each one missed costs a little. They show on the Race weekend tab and are judged on the end-of-season screen, which also lists the winter's market moves.
- Old careers load with the original grid and a two-year contract. `tests/career.mjs` now has 62 checks, including eight simulated winters: the grid stays valid, drivers retire and are replaced, the career grid is the one raced, and contracts and goals survive a save and load.

### Latest: AI strategy and race control (SPEC-v2 sections 3 and 7)
- **Stint planner (7.1).** Each AI car gets a strategy at the start of any race of 8 laps or more. The planner weighs no-stop, one-stop and two-stop plans over the dry compounds, keeps every stint within the tyre's life and respects a mandatory-stop rule. Each plan is costed as compound pace deficit plus degradation plus about 22 s a stop, and a car takes one of the plans within 6 s of the best, so the field splits its strategies. After each stop the next stint follows the plan, shifted if the stop came early or late, and a safety car or VSC brings a cheap stop forward (wear over 32 %). Over two 10-lap races at Vettore: 3 car contacts against 37 with the old random pit window, and about 125 position changes a race. Sprints under 8 laps keep the old varied start tyres. With the planner they all chose the same no-stop hard tyre and the race became a procession (5 position changes in four races).
- **ERS saving is back on (3.2).** A car that is saving its battery now gives up the plan the moment a car closes fast behind (inside 0.65 s and more than 2.5 m/s quicker). Before that guard it nearly doubled car contact; with it, 11 against 7 at Elite and 11 against 10 at F1 over 4 seeds, inside the spread between seeds.
- **Blue flags (3.4).** The yield weight goes from 3.2 to 4.2, and inside 65 m a lapped car lifts (86 %) and moves clearly to the side of the road the leader is not using.
- **Cold out-lap (3.4).** On the lap after a stop the AI drives to the grip its cold new tyres actually have (up to 9 % gentler) until they reach their window.
- **Corner-exit tyre care (7.2).** With rears above their window, the AI feeds the throttle in up to 35 % more gently out of corners.
- **Breaking the tow (7.2).** A defender covering on a long straight (more than 220 m to the corner) may make one clean 2.2 m diagonal move to break the attacker's slipstream, then holds the line.
- **Corner cutting (7.3).** Leaving the track and being more than 0.25 s quicker through the next two checkpoints than your best is a 5 s penalty at once, on top of the usual three-warnings rule.
- **Qualifying traffic (7.4).** A car on its out-lap, on an invalidated lap or on its cool-down treats a car on a flying lap like a blue flag.
- **Greasy line in the rain.** In the wet the rubbered racing line now has up to 5 % less grip than the tarmac beside it.
- **Not done: the wet line (3.1).** The spec's version (shift the line by `-sign(lineD) × 2.4 m`) jumps the path 5 m sideways wherever the racing line crosses the centre of the road, and the AI spun. A smooth version still had to take a tighter path than the speed profile was built for, and measured 4 s a lap slower at Rade and 30 s at Northfield in heavy rain. It is in the code behind `RC_TUNE.wetLine` and switched off.

`tests/physics2.mjs` now has 36 checks; all 27 headless suites pass.

### Latest: skip straight to the results; virtual safety car
- **No more waiting at the end of a race.** Finishing used to mean watching a cool-down lap and then a fast-forward of the rest of the field, which could take a minute or more. The finish card now has a **Results ▶** button (Enter or a click do the same) from 0.6 s after the flag, and it moves on by itself after 14 s. The cars still running are given the finish they were on course for (the rest of their current lap at their recent pace, `Race.finishNow`) instead of being raced home. On a 20-car test race the estimated classification matched the fully raced one in all 20 positions. A player whose car has retired can press Enter to skip the rest of the race too.
- **Virtual safety car (SPEC 3.3).** One or two stranded or retired cars now bring out a VSC (after 3 s of yellow) for 18-28 s and until the track is clear; three or more still bring out the real safety car, and a VSC turns into one if more cars stop. There is no pace car: every driver must hold 62 % of the reference speed. The AI holds 60 %. The timing panel shows your **VSC delta** (green at or below zero, red when too fast); more than 1.5 s over for 5 s, or at the end, is a 5 s penalty. DRS is off, and the AI takes a cheap pit stop under the VSC as it does under the safety car. Measured in `tests/physics2.mjs`: the field slows to about 60 % of its pace (148 against 249 km/h), the VSC comes in and goes, a player who ignores it is penalised and one who slows down is not.

### Latest: v2 physics (SPEC-v2 sections 1 and 5)
Each feature has a switch in `PHYS` (`src/sim/car.js`) so it can be measured on its own; all are on. `tests/physics2.mjs` (24 checks) covers them.

- **Suspension and ground effect (1.1).** Every corner has a spring (180 kN/m front, 160 kN/m rear) and a damper (12 kNs/m) driven by its load, so the car has a real ride height: about 8 mm at the front and 13 mm at the rear at 320 km/h, from 45 / 65 mm at rest. Downforce follows the ride height (up to +18 % lower down) and the rake (more front wing load nose-down). One deliberate change from the spec: the gain is measured against the ride height the car would have at that speed on a flat road, so it is the *change* that matters (kerbs, crests, compressions, dive under braking, squat). Applied raw, every car would gain about 18 % downforce at speed and every lap time and AI calibration would move. Straight-line speed is unchanged to 0.1 km/h. When the floor reaches the road it stops there, scrapes (400 N of plank friction, sparks) and the CarView shows the real spring travel instead of an estimate.
- **Porpoising (5.2).** Running the rear under 12 mm above 260 km/h stalls the diffuser (-32 % rear downforce) until the car has risen to 18 mm again. With the standard set-up this only happens over compressions.
- **Flat-spot hop and self-aligning torque (1.2).** A flat-spotted tyre pulses its corner load once a revolution while it rolls (not when locked: a locked tyre has nothing to hop on). `car.sat` (front lateral force times a pneumatic trail that collapses past the slip peak) and `car.flatShake` are exposed for steering feel.
- **Marbles (1.3).** The rubber each car wears off is swept off the racing line into five bins across the road, mostly at the corners; the line itself stays clean. A tyre that runs over them loses up to 11 % grip, picks some up, carries it for a few corners and heats its surface. After five laps of a 20-car race at Northfield the worst spots are at 0.3 (2.7 % grip), more with high tyre wear or a long race.
- **Launch clutch and launch control (1.4, 7.3).** The clutch is let in over a quarter of a second at lights out. A new *Launch control* assist (on by default, off in the Simulator preset): off, you hold the car on the brake during the countdown and set the revs with the throttle. Over 11,800 rpm the rears light up; under 6,500 the anti-stall cuts in and the car bogs down (about 0.1 s lost; the spec's 0.6 s anti-stall cost too little to notice, so it lasts 0.9 s); moving before the lights go out is a 10 s false-start penalty. With launch control on, 0-100 km/h is still 2.64 s.
- **Reliability (1.5).** Revs in the rich mix stress the power unit (misfiring from 75 %, failure at 100 %: about 20 minutes of flat-out rich running), cooked brakes stress the brake lines (a failure makes one front brake weak and the car pulls), over-revved shifts stress the gearbox. The engineer warns you at 50 % and 75 %. In normal racing no car comes close (0.012 after a 5-lap race). A failed car **retires**: it coasts to a stop, is classified behind every running car with no points and "DNF · Power unit" on the results, and the marshals wheel it away after a few seconds (a stopped car first brings out the yellow flag).
- **Banking (5.1).** Every corner is banked into the turn by up to 2.9°, smoothed along the lap, level on the pit straight; a circuit can override stretches (`def.banking`). The spec asked for up to 8° (curvature × 18): that is oval-track banking, real road circuits run 1-3°, and the road mesh, kerbs, walls, run-off, racing-line guide and the cars all tilt with it. Gravity along the camber adds cornering force, the AI's speed profile knows it, and **every lap is now about 0.3-0.6 % quicker**: the pace table and the game's rival-pace hint are regenerated.
- **Two-node brakes and glazing (5.3).** The friction face (what you see glowing, what fades) runs above the disc core by the power going through it; dirty air cools the ducts less well. Dragging cool brakes lightly glazes the pads (-15 % initial bite) until the next hard stop.
- **Fuel (5.4).** As the tank empties the CG moves forward (+1.2 % front weight) and the fuel sloshes behind the car's own load transfer in long corners.
- **Punctures (5.5).** A puncture now deflates, slowly or fast, instead of being instantly flat: the rolling radius shrinks, grip falls by up to 65 %, the car pulls towards the flat side and runs on the rim in a stream of sparks.

**Measured:** all 27 headless suites pass. Racing quality at Northfield over 4 seeds against the build before: Elite 5 → 13 car contacts, 14 → 12 wall hits, 4 → 2 recoveries; F1 39 → 23 contacts, 46 → 54 wall hits, 19 → 21 recoveries. Those are within the spread between seeds; the new physics neither cleaned the racing up nor made it worse.

### Latest: the career, rebuilt around the driver
The career was a list of races with a budget attached: finish well, buy one of three upgrades, and at the end of the season the standings alone decided whether a better team called. It now has a driver in it.

- **Reputation** (0 to 100, from Rookie to Legend). Each race moves it: by how far you beat or missed the team's target, by whether you finished ahead of your team-mate, with a bonus for a podium or a win and for the fastest lap, and a cost for a penalty or a retirement. A short season moves it as much as a long one. The season's verdict adds more (a title, a top three, the constructors' target met or missed).
- **Contracts.** Every team wants a certain reputation before it offers a seat (85 for the best car, nothing for the slowest). The best three offers you have earned are on the table at the end of the season; a standout championship position still earns one on its own, as before. A seat can now also be lost: finish a season 18 or more below what your team expects without meeting the constructors' target and the contract is not renewed, with only lesser teams to choose from.
- **A retainer.** The team pays you after every race whatever the result: 2 a race at the slowest team, 8 at the best. Moving up is now worth money as well as speed.
- **Sponsor challenges.** Before each race three are offered and you may accept one: finish without a penalty, beat your team-mate, gain three places, beat the target by two, set the fastest lap, finish on the podium, win. Harder ones pay more; nothing is lost by failing. Goals the car is expected to deliver anyway are not offered. The three are fixed for the round, so reopening the hub does not reroll them.
- **Your team-mate.** The other car of the team is a named driver; the hub keeps the head-to-head in races and in qualifying, and every result says where they finished.
- **Six areas of development instead of three.** Power unit, aerodynamics and chassis as before, plus a hybrid system (+5 % battery capacity a level), tyre management (-4 % tyre wear a level) and a pit crew (-0.2 s a stop per level). All three reach the car in the race (`tests/career.mjs` checks the battery and the wear rate on the player's car and on no other).
- **Rival teams develop.** Every winter each team's car gains or loses a little (at most 0.18 on a rating that runs from 0.15 to 1.0, half of last year's form carried over, the field as a whole neither gaining nor losing). The development tab shows every car's strength this season with an arrow for the teams that moved.
- **The team's target follows the size of the grid.** P14 was the target for a midfield car even in an eight-car race, where it could not be missed; it now scales with the number of cars.
- **A new hub** with five tabs: *Race weekend* (the circuit, forecast, target, retainer, the sponsor's offers, the head-to-head and the paddock's headlines about your last races), *Calendar* (every round with its forecast, your result, your points and the winner), *Development*, *Standings* (complete tables), *Driver* (reputation with the mark your team expects, the reputation every team asks for, career record: races, wins, podiums, poles, fastest laps, points, best finish, titles; and past seasons). The result screen of a career race shows the headline, the target, the team-mate, each source of money and the change in reputation.
- **Old careers keep working.** A career saved before this loads with the reputation its team implies, the three new development areas at zero, an empty record and every team at its base strength; junk in any of the new fields is cleaned on load.

What was not done: a career race weekend is still qualifying and a race (no practice sessions), the sponsor challenges are one race long (no season-long sponsors), and nobody has balanced the economy over a long career by playing one. The numbers above are first settings, checked for sense on a simulated four-race season, not tuned.

### Latest: a new look (cars, trees, sky, pit building)
The game looked like it was built from boxes because much of it was. This round replaces the worst of that.

- **The car is a new model.** The old one was a smooth tube with boxes attached: slab wings, box mirrors, a box floor, box suspension arms, flat discs for wheel faces. The new one is built from a small geometry toolkit (`src/render/carshape.js`):
  - *bodywork* lofted through spline-joined cross-sections, with separate roundness for the top and bottom of each section and an undercut: a drooping nose that lands on the front wing, a flat-bottomed tub, sidepods with a tall inlet and the body tucked in beneath it, an airbox and engine-cover spine, a tail that tightens into the "coke bottle" between the rear wheels;
  - *wings* that are real airfoil sections swept along a curved span: a four-element front wing that sweeps back and rises towards its endplates, a spoon-shaped rear wing with a separate DRS flap, a two-element beam wing, a swan-neck pylon;
  - *plates* with smooth outlines and rounded edges: curved front endplates, two-tone rear endplates, the engine-cover fin, a floor that is wide ahead of the rear wheels and tucked in between them, tunnel fences, a floor-edge lip, a diffuser with fences;
  - *suspension* as aero-section wishbones, push rods, track rods and drive shafts that still follow the wheels as the suspension works;
  - *wheels* with low-profile tyres (bulging sidewall, rounded shoulders), dished wheel covers with a bright lip and a centre nut, and the tyre compound as a thin stripe on the sidewall instead of a painted ring; rubber is matt and the covers satin metal in the same mesh;
  - a halo, mirrors on stalks, a helmet with a visor and a stripe, a T-cam pod, an exhaust and a rain light.
  From the driver's seat the eye point is now where the driver's eyes are (it was 17 cm above the helmet), the steering wheel sits in the cockpit instead of floating over it, and the halo is drawn as its slim centre pillar only.
- **Three levels of car detail** with the same shape: about 25,000 triangles for the car you drive, 13,400 for the rest of the grid on High, and a lighter mesh still on Medium and Low (the old car was 5,900). `tests/carshape.mjs` checks that every builder produces a closed, consistently wound, outward-facing solid.
- **Trees are no longer faceted.** Broadleaf crowns are lumpy overlapping lobes shaded as one soft volume (smooth normals that lean away from the middle of the crown, darker underneath and inside, lighter on top); conifers are overlapping skirts of drooping, ragged-edged branches; palms have arching, folded fronds and a leaning trunk. The trees nearest the road get a finer mesh than the ones behind them, and every tree has a soft patch of shade on the ground under it, because they are too many to put in the shadow map and floated without one.
- **The sky is a sky.** It was three-quarters covered in flat grey cloud on a dry day. Now: separate cumulus with bright edges and shaded bases over a clear blue, a band of haze at the horizon, and cloud that thickens into a real overcast as rain arrives. The image-based lighting on the cars comes from this sky, so the paint picks up the blue as well.
- **The pit building** has an upper storey of dark glass that mirrors the sky, mullions and an overhanging roof.
- **Colour grade**: a little more saturation and contrast after tone mapping.
- **Cost, measured** (one frame, 20 cars on the grid at Northfield, shadow pass included): 2.1 million triangles on High against 1.3 million before, 1.6 against 1.15 million on Medium. Draw calls are almost unchanged (1,754 against 1,686). The first version of this work was 3.6 million; the detail levels above brought it down. This was counted, not timed: the test machine renders in software, so there is no frame-rate measurement. The automatic quality scaler still drops a level if the frame rate falls.

### Latest: why rivals crashed, traced and fixed
A new tool, `tools/incident-causes.mjs`, records for every marshal recovery what happened to that car in the ten seconds before it, and what first damaged it. It showed that rivals almost never ran off on their own on the racing line; the recoveries came from three specific faults:

- **Defending rivals braked for the wrong corner.** The speed profile is for the racing line, which uses the whole width of the road. A rival that moved to the inside to cover or block kept braking for that speed on a much tighter arc, arrived too fast, ran out of steering lock and spun into the wall, then limped round being recovered again and again. Drivers now work out the corner speed of the path they are actually on (`AIDriver.offLineV`, switch `RC_TUNE.offLine`) and brake for that. A defender or an attacker on the inside is now slower through the corner than a car on the line, which is also how it works in real racing.
- **A recovered car could be stranded outside the pit wall.** A car that was put back on the road while heading for its pit box stayed in the "pitting" state on the circuit side of the pit wall and drove at the wall until it was recovered again, every nine seconds. It now rejoins in the pit lane only if it was already in it, and otherwise abandons the stop.
- **Badly damaged cars spun on the pit entry.** A car with a puncture, or with its wings off and its tyres worn through, took the entry ramp at the limiter speed and spun. It now crawls in at 60 % of the limit until it is straight in the lane. Healthy cars are unaffected.

Measured on Northfield (20 cars, 5 laps, triple tyre wear so that everybody has to stop), summed over the seeds given, with the off-line corner speed switched off and on in otherwise identical code:

| Level | Seeds | Car contacts | Wall hits | Marshal recoveries | Position changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elite | 8 | 57 → 26 | 152 → 103 | 48 → 15 | 1069 → 848 |
| Legend | 4 | 44 → 21 | 124 → 24 | 40 → 14 | 640 → 455 |
| F1 | 8 | 64 → 58 | 218 → 185 | 76 → 71 | 1042 → 958 |
| Legend, Vettore | 4 | 46 → 36 | 32 → 48 | 13 → 7 | 530 → 519 |

What this does and does not show: Elite and Legend are clearly cleaner. F1 level is not: the difference there is inside the noise (one set of four seeds was worse, the other much better). There are 10 to 25 % fewer position changes, partly because fewer cars are crashing out of the way and partly because a car off the line is now honestly slower.

- **Rivals drive to their weaker axle.** What was left at F1 level was cars on worn tyres still being driven at F1 pace: a driver judged his grip from the average of the four tyres, but a car with dead rears and fair fronts lets go at the rear. A rival lapping alone at F1 level on Northfield spun on its fifth lap for exactly this reason. Drivers now take the weaker of the two axles (`RC_TUNE.weakAxle`). On top of the change above, F1 level over the same 8 seeds: car contacts 58 → 51, wall hits 185 → 80, marshal recoveries 71 → 32, finishers 152 → 159 of 160. Elite over 4 seeds: contacts 7 → 5, wall hits 20 → 14, recoveries 5 → 4, finishers 77 → 80 of 80. The cost, and it is a real one: a rival's first laps on fresh tyres are unchanged (the pace table is the same to a hundredth), but it now fades as its tyres go. Alone on mediums at Legend level on Northfield, lap six is 1:14.9 instead of 1:13.9. The rivals are slower late in a stint than they were; in exchange they stop crashing.

- **Rival pace next to your own.** The race setup screen now says how fast the rivals lap the chosen circuit at the chosen difficulty (measured, the README pace table), compares it with your record there, and offers **Match my pace**, which sets the level whose rivals lap as fast as you do. `tests/pacetable.mjs` keeps the table in step with the circuits and difficulty presets; `JSON=file node tools/pace-table.mjs` regenerates it.
- **Checked by eye for the first time:** the sector bridges and the marshal posts with their yellow flags were shipped without ever being looked at; both now have screenshots and render correctly. Still not seen in a screenshot: bottoming sparks. Still not heard by anybody: the audio mix.

### Racing, rules and AI
- **AI recalibrated against human lap times** (Hautes-Fagnes 1:19, Vettore 1:08.5, Rade 1:06.4): brakes late and hard instead of bleeding speed over a long distance, speed envelope accounts for lateral load transfer, the racing line is smoothed so it can be driven at 340 km/h, longer steering preview. Legend: Vettore 1:08.0, Rade 1:05.3, Hautes-Fagnes 1:17.8, Northfield 1:22.3. Elite about 3 % slower, Pro 7 %, Amateur 13 %, Rookie 20 %.
- **Racecraft**: drivers now draft behind the car in front and pull out to pass, choose the inside of the next corner, defend once with a single move to cover the attacker's side (never weaving, never under braking), refuse to move into a gap another car is taking, hold their line when a car is alongside, and stay on the track. Following cars brake early enough to stop behind a slower or braking car, take corners a little slower in dirty air, and drive to what a damaged or punctured car has left.
- **Identical cars** option (default on): every team has exactly the player's car; only the drivers differ. Off keeps the team pecking order (career always uses it).
- **Safety car**: three or more stranded cars deploy the pace car (yellow flag first for a single stranded car). The field queues behind it, DRS and boost are off, overtaking behind it earns the player a 5 s penalty, cars with worn tyres pit cheaply, and it comes in when the track has been clear for a while, followed by a green flag.
- **Weather**: Dry, Light rain, Heavy rain or Changeable (a dry start with a shower). Rain cuts slick-tyre grip by about 30 % and makes kerbs slippery; the new Wet tyre gives the grip back (and runs cool, but wears and overheats on a dry track). Tyres cool faster on a wet track. AI cars start on and switch to the right tyres, brake earlier and are less aggressive in the wet. Radio calls for the forecast, a wet track and a drying track.
- **Time of day**: midday to dusk over the race (sky, sun colour and angle, longer shadows, exposure), or a full night race with stars, floodlight masts, pools of light on the road and the player's headlights.
- **Interactive pit stops**: press the DRS key as each of the four wheel-nut markers enters the green. A flawless stop is 2.5 s, a so-so press 0.12 s, a cross-threaded nut costs 0.9 s (toggle in Assists).
- **Damage model**: sidepods are separate structures that crumple on side impacts (visible deformation), front and rear wings bend and break, floor damage reduces downforce. The HUD shows wing / sidepod / rear wing health and the downforce lost, heavy damage sets a pit request automatically (B cancels), and repairs add time to the stop in proportion to the damage. AI pit for any severe damage.
- **Tyres**: a lock-up now flat-spots the tyre, spikes its temperature (capped) and accelerates its wear; flat spots cost grip until the tyres are changed.
- **Stewards**: running into the back of the car ahead at speed is a "causing a collision" penalty (5 s, then 10 s, then 20 s for the player; 5 s for AI).
- **DRS** verified end to end: only inside a zone, only after crossing the detection line within 1.0 s of the car ahead, never on lap 1, in the pit lane, under the safety car or in a corner. Painted detection and activation lines and DRS / DRS DETECTION / DRS END signs at every zone.
- **Slipstream** verified: about +15 % extra acceleration 12 m behind and +10 % at 20 m at 270 km/h.
- In-race tuning keys: **X** ERS mode (harvest / balanced / attack), **Z** fuel mix (lean / standard / rich), **[ ]** live brake bias.
- **Pit-entry fixes (changeable weather)**: cars now slow to the pit limit *before* the entry ramp starts turning (they used to brake and steer at the same time and spin on the ramp), call the pits for wet tyres as soon as rain arrives rather than once the track is soaked, abandon a missed entry and try again next lap, and a car wedged in the pit lane is recovered by the marshals like any other. A changeable-weather race went from about 80 marshal recoveries to about 20; the whole field finishes.
- Safety car: a car heading for the pits now obeys the pace-car speed cap until it reaches the pit lane.
- **Pit-wall strategy**: the race setup has a Pit wall option (Silent / Radio advice / Calls you in). The engineers read your actual tyre wear, project it to the flag, and call the lap to box on and the tyre to fit (wets when the track is soaked, slicks when it dries, the mandatory stop before the last laps). The tyre panel shows the box lap, and in the last two laps nothing is called unless the weather demands it. `tests/strategy.mjs` covers it.
- **Tyre pressure setup** (Team & car tab, -3..+3): higher pressure heats the tyres faster and wears them more slowly, lower pressure grips better in the wet; the standard setting is the dry sweet spot. **Live telemetry** (key Y): the last twelve seconds of speed, throttle, brake and steering as traces on screen.
- **Brake discs**: each wheel now has a disc temperature (heated by braking power, cooled by speed). Cold carbon bites less, above about 880 C it fades (up to 30 % less torque), and the discs glow orange to white through the wheels from 450 C. The tyre panel shows the hottest disc and the radio warns before fade; street circuits (Rade, Porto Vecchio) cook the brakes most. Typical peaks are 800-1150 C; lap times are unchanged.
- **Career forecasts**: every round of a career season has a fixed forecast (dry, showers possible, light or heavy rain), shown on the career hub; wet-weather rounds start the player on wets.
- **Fuel is burnt per distance, not per second**. A slow driver, a car waiting for the marshals or one stuck in a queue used to run the tank dry before the flag (a 5-lap race at slow pace ran out of fuel and cars stopped on hills). The tank loaded for the race distance now always lasts, and a field of 20 at Rookie level went from 105 marshal recoveries to none.
- **Longer pit-entry and exit ramps**: the sideways move into the pit lane is now spread over a distance that keeps the lateral load under about 0.75 g at the pit limit (about 85 m instead of 45-60 m), so cars no longer spin at the entry, and the lane geometry on every circuit follows.
- **AI tyre strategy**: starting compounds are chosen so the first stint covers at least 45 % of the race; stops are called from the wear actually measured over the last completed lap (the first lap on a set counts for 80 %, launch and warm-up included); the next set is the softest one that lasts to the flag at the chosen wear setting; the old wait-for-the-cliff rule is a last resort. A 20-car Legend race went from about 150 recoveries to under 12.
- The AI now brakes for the discs it actually has (cold carbon bites less, cooked carbon fades), and chooses starting compounds that last about half the race with a margin; wall impacts in a 20-car Legend race fell from 30-50 to under 10.
- Top-level AI keep about 2 % in hand in a race compared with a hot lap (qualifying and time trial are unchanged).

### Robustness
- Ninth review round (HUD, track): a new session starts with a clean HUD (no leftover finish banner, confetti, start lights or telemetry trace); the pit-stop panel reports the real stop time; overlapping HUD timers no longer hide a fresh key hint or the start lights; driver and team names are escaped in the finish banner; the pit panel only rebuilds when its text changes; track generation cannot loop forever when a kerb or run-off range ends on the last sample.
- Realism pass, graphics and trackside: the road is now shaded per pixel. After rain the **racing line dries first** (patchily, with puddles and reflections staying off line) and the dry line is real: a car on it has more grip than one off it. **Rubber builds up** on the line over a race (darker, with a sheen, and up to 1.2 % more grip in the dry). Asphalt and kerbs are **bump-mapped**, so low sun and floodlights pick out the grain and the saw-tooth kerb ridges. Tyres have rounded shoulders instead of being plain cylinders. Cars gain bargeboards, floor strakes and edges, diffuser fences, front-wing cascades and dive planes, and a **steering wheel with a live display** (shift lights, gear, speed, battery, DRS, position) that turns with your steering in the cockpit and T-cam views. Trackside: a **bridge over the track at each sector line**, braking boards on **both** sides of the road, and **marshal posts** every 350 m whose flags wave yellow under a caution or safety car, green at the start and chequered at the finish. Post-processing adds **heat haze** behind the exhausts of the nearest four cars and a soft **sun glare** with a streak when the low sun is in shot (hidden when something is in front of it).
- Realism pass, the car on the road: **live suspension** - each corner's spring travel follows its real vertical load, so the body squats under power, dives under braking, leans in corners, sits down at speed and the wheels work over kerbs while the wishbones swing to follow; wheels stay on the road instead of pitching with the body. **Crests and compressions** are in the physics: the car goes light over a crest and is pressed into the road in a dip (Sakura runs from 0.70 g to 1.35 g), the speed profile and the rivals' braking know about it, and gravity along a slope lengthens or shortens braking. **Floor bottoming**: at very high speed or in a fast compression the plank touches down, with a shower of sparks from under the rear of the car and a scrape you can hear.
- Realism pass, tyres: each tyre now has a fast **surface** temperature on top of the slow **carcass** temperature. The surface flashes 15-20 K above the carcass in a hard corner and 100 K in a lock-up, and is cooled by the airflow on the straights; the carcass still sets the grip window and the degradation, so lap times are unchanged (within 0.05 s on four circuits). A cooked surface costs grip at once. **Graining**: pushing on cold rubber tears the surface and costs up to 7 % grip until warm laps clean it up - most likely on the lap after a pit stop. The HUD tyre colours follow the surface and it warns when the tyres are graining. The outside tyres of a lap wear measurably faster (Northfield: 28.4 % against 25.3 %). `tests/tyres.mjs`.
- Realism pass, rivals: **multi-lap energy plan** - a rival stuck behind a car of the same pace stops wasting its battery, harvests for a lap or two, then spends all of it down one long straight with DRS and the tow. **Undercut and overcut**: inside its pit window a rival stuck within 1.5 s of the car ahead stops early for clean air, and one whose rival has just pitted stays out a lap longer if its tyres have life (one car at a time, never into a busy pit lane - unrationed, 11 cars tried it on the same lap). **Switchback**: an attacker boxed out on the outside at the turn-in slows in and cuts back under the rival for the exit instead of lifting. 20-car race at F1 level: Rade 77 passes with no contact; Northfield 103 passes, 7 car contacts.
- Realism pass, sound: a **gearbox whine** pitched by shaft speed and loudest under load, a **turbo whistle** that spools with boost, **wastegate flutter** when you lift off at full boost, and the floor scraping.
- Measured and not adopted: an asymmetric (late-apex) racing line. Two versions were tried - sliding slow-corner apexes 4-12 m later, and weighting the solver to straighten exits at the cost of entries. The mildest exit-weighting was level with the symmetric line and everything stronger was slower, on six circuits (by up to 1.3 s a lap), so the symmetric line stays; the exit-weighting remains in the solver as a switched-off tunable (`LINE_TUNE` in `src/sim/track.js`).
- A better racing line. The old line was a minimum-curvature solution smoothed so heavily (24 m) and tied so strongly to path length that it hardly used the width of the track. It is now a full-width, late-apex line (path-length weight 0.001, 10 m smoothing), and its quality was measured, not assumed: the AI drives each candidate line in the real physics simulation. Result on all 12 circuits: laps 7-12 % faster with no track-limit violations (Rade 65.4 s to 59.3 s). Because a faster line is no use if you cannot stay on the road, it is kept 1.9 m inside the edge (2.6 m on street circuits, where it also stays 3.2 m from the barrier), and rivals keep their planned passing positions 2.0 m (2.8 m between walls) from the edge; at that setting the 20-car field races cleanly on Rade, Porto and Lumiere. The guide ribbon starts under your car and eases onto the line over 55 m (it used to jump to the line), widens with distance so it stays readable, and has a white bar at each braking point and a square at each apex. Rivals ride the same line, so every difficulty level is about 9 % quicker than before (README pace table regenerated); to keep the straights honest the DRS / straight check now tolerates the line's gentle lateral sweep. Solver parameters can be tuned with `computeRacingLine(track, margin, lengthWeight, smoothLen)`.
- Career rules: a career now has editable rules, on the New Career screen and, at any time, from the hub's *Career rules* button. Race rules: **laps per race** (0 follows the distance preset, 1-30 fixes it, so 1 makes every round a one-lap sprint), cars on the grid (2-20), your start position without qualifying, weather (each round's forecast, or always dry / light rain / heavy rain / changeable), tyre wear (off, low, normal, high), pit-wall help, damage, collisions, a mandatory stop and adaptive AI pace. Season rules: **rounds per season** (1-24; a long season goes round the circuits again), race distance, AI difficulty (now including F1, and it stays where you set it), whether the rivals improve every season, and a **prize-money multiplier** (x0.5 to x5) for race prizes and the season bonus. - Rival racecraft, a full decision matrix (`src/sim/racecraft.js`): every rival now thinks ten times a second. Perception turns the traffic into ten 0..1 features (how tightly it sits behind the car ahead, its pace edge, braking zone, corner proximity, straight, room beside the target, DRS, a threat behind, a lapping leader, risk from rain, damage and penalties). Seven actions each have a row of weights in a matrix - LINE, FOLLOW, DRAFT, ATTACK, COVER, BLOCK, YIELD - scaled by the driver's own aggression / defence / risk-tolerance; impossible actions are masked, the current one gets a hysteresis bonus, the best wins. What they do with it: they sit in your tow on the straights, tuck onto the inside of the next corner, dive at the braking zone with a later brake point (up to 11 % more brake demand for a committed attack), abandon a dive that has not got its nose alongside by the turn-in, cover your line on a straight with one move, take the inside before the braking zone to close the door, and pull aside for a lapping leader with a lift. A human rival gets more room alongside than an AI car. Low-level drivers (novices) never defend. `RaceBrain.explain()` returns every score, and with the live telemetry open (Y) the HUD shows the matrix of the car ahead and the car behind you. `tests/brain.mjs` pins the decisions for 20 situations.
- Rivals use the car properly: they run their own ERS mode (attack mode in a fight, harvest while stuck in a car's wake, a last-laps flat-out deploy), their own fuel mix (lean when short, rich in a fight) from a measured fuel burn per lap, open the ERS boost when attacking or defending, and on the straights they no longer stop at the plain profile speed: with the DRS flap open or the boost on they keep accelerating (+5 % / +2 % target speed), like the car physically can. Traffic awareness reaches 190 m ahead and scales with the closing speed, so a car crawling into the pit lane at 300 km/h of closing speed is seen in time. A new **F1** difficulty (level 1.15: flawless, relentless, near-zero mistakes) tops the presets. `tools/racing-quality.mjs` counts passes, contacts, recoveries and penalties for a 20-car race: against the old line-followers, wall hits fell from 112 to 55, recoveries from 43 to 32 and car contacts from 56 to 47 on three circuits.
- Rivals judge traffic by where it is going, not only where it is: each car tracks the lateral speed of its neighbours, a car merging onto the line (a pit exit at 80 km/h with 300 km/h of closing speed, a defender moving across) counts as a hazard before it arrives (a wider reach for pit-exit cars), a dive is dropped when the car ahead has moved across and closed the gap, and nobody tucks in behind a crawling car. Race punctures are now deterministic so a replayed race is the same race. With these, the 20-car field at F1 level has 2-3 car contacts and no wall hits or recoveries on Rade and Northfield (`tools/racing-quality.mjs`).
- The blanket speed-up for every car was taken back out (it only made the same race faster): the stock car again launches 0-100 km/h in about 2.6 s and every car, rivals included, is identical (`CAR_BASE` is neutral). *Standard* is the default for your car again; *Quick* (+8 % power, firmer launch) and *Rocket* (+30 %) stay available as a handicap for starting at the back.
